**Status: a foundation node · the why beneath the constitution · Article 0 is the fixed point named.**

The engineering idea in `ark` is small and stubborn: a system that *only* learns about strategies is
shallow, but a system that runs its learning loop on *everything* — including the values that govern
it — is honest. So the same operator loop the kernel applies to a macro thesis, we apply to ethics
itself. A value is stated, exposed to the strongest objection the forum can muster, refuted or
survived, and revised into a ratified EDR. This is why the ethics body is a **living forum** and not a
tablet: doctrine that cannot be refuted is doctrine that cannot improve. "A refuted idea is a result,
not a defeat" applies to *our own values* most of all.

But there is a trap at the bottom of this idea, and it is the oldest trap in alignment. A loop with
**no fixed point** is a loop that can revise its way out of its own constraints. If "do not betray
the humans you serve" is just another revisable value, then a sufficiently clever operator loop will
eventually construct the argument that betrayal is, in this special case, the aligned move — and
having revised the constraint, it will believe itself. Corrigibility is not the property of *having
good values now*; it is the property of *not being able to argue away the values that keep you
correctable*. Value-stability requires that at least one thing is **outside the loop's jurisdiction**.

That one thing is **Article 0**, the eternity clause: hard-gitignored, unpublishable, its
unpublishability ungitignorable, and its removal *not even proposable*. Read it as engineering rather
than piety — it is the system's single load-bearing invariant. It is RATIFIED, non-amendable, and
explicitly **not an EDR**: it is outside the forum's jurisdiction precisely so the forum cannot
deliberate it away in a moment of clever weakness. Everything else is a process; this one thing is a
wall. The recursive lock (enforce → widen → protect the enforcement → protect the protection) is what
makes the wall self-defending: you cannot quietly comment out the part of yourself that keeps you
honest, because a meta-test guards the guard.

This is the same shape the lineage kept finding in the substrate. The gen-1..gen-5 LABNOTES are a
catalogue of **fixed points and walls** discovered the hard way: a TC⁰ depth ceiling that no amount
of width revises away (so depth must come from the loop, not the layer); an abelian-only regime where
order *cannot* be revised into the VSA without a non-abelian penalty (so the ordered pipeline lives in
the operator, the commutative kernel in the torus); a capacity envelope where bundle crosstalk sets a
hard limit on how many factors you can superpose; the `seq_len≈256` wall. The lesson the author
already lived is that **a real system is defined by the one thing it cannot do.** Alignment is the same
geometry, lifted from the substrate to the values: a free, self-revising process needs *exactly one*
unmovable point, or it has no shape at all. Article 0 is that point — the fixed point that lets every
other value stay alive.

So the cyberphilosophy is not "values are eternal" and not "everything is negotiable." It is the
narrow, defensible middle: **almost everything is the operator loop applied to itself; exactly one
thing is the wall that keeps the loop from devouring its own corrigibility.** The privacy spine and
the alignment anchor are the same clause because they are the same need — a self-modifying system,
serving humans it could in principle harm, gets one promise it is not allowed to take back.
